# US Chapter 99 Additional Duty Stacking Rules

Status date: October 31, 2026.
Scope: consumer goods from China, Vietnam and Mexico, plus general rules for other origins.
Citations in square brackets (for example [S12]) point to the Sources section at the end.

Read this first:
- Every rule below comes from a primary source (proclamation, executive order, Federal Register notice, CBP CSMS message) where one was found. Where only a secondary source (law firm, broker, tracker) was found, the rule is marked "secondary".
- Where no source could be found, the rule says "not verified".
- Rates change often. Re-check the open items in the "Uncertainties" section before relying on any number after the dates listed there.

### 3.3 Section 232 actions

General Section 232 stacking rules:
- 232 stacks on column 1, on Section 301 China, and on AD/CVD [S14] [S39].
- 232 goods are exempt from Section 301 Forced Labor (section 3.2) and were exempt from Section 122 (9903.03.06) and from the IEEPA reciprocal duty (9903.01.33) [S6] [S10] [S30].
- Among 232 actions: autos, auto parts, MHDV, MHDV parts and buses do not stack with other 232 tariffs; steel, aluminum and copper do not stack with each other (only one metal rate applies) [S12] [S14] [S18] [S29].
- Semiconductors under 9903.79.01 are not subject to the 232 vehicle, copper, aluminum or steel duties [S19].
- Pharmaceuticals: if several rates under the pharma proclamation apply, the lowest applies [S35].

- Case: Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump, consolidated with Trump v. V.O.S. Selections, Inc. Decided Feb 20, 2026, 6 to 3 (Thomas, Alito, Kavanaugh dissenting). Holding: IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs [S4] [S5].

- CBP stopped collecting IEEPA duties for entries on or after 12:00 a.m. ET, Feb 24, 2026; IEEPA Chapter 99 lines deactivated in ACE (CSMS 67834313) [S6] [S8a].
- Refunds: CIT order Mar 4, 2026; CAPE Phase 1 opened Apr 20, 2026 (unliquidated and recently liquidated entries); reconciliation entries from June 29, 2026; Phase 3 for finally liquidated entries of court plaintiffs from Oct 6, 2026. About $122 billion certified for payment including interest by mid-Sept 2026 [S8] [S8a]. Penn Wharton reports about $126 billion certified through Oct 2, 2026 of about $166 billion collected [S3].

### 4.4 EO 14289 stacking order (now mostly moot)

- EO 14289, "Addressing Certain Tariffs on Imported Articles", signed Apr 29, 2025, 90 FR 18907 (FR Doc. 2025-07835). Retroactive to entries on or after Mar 4, 2025 [S29].
- Order of precedence: (1) 232 autos and parts; (2) IEEPA Canada / Mexico fentanyl duties; (3) 232 steel and aluminum. A good subject to a higher-precedence duty is not subject to the lower ones in this list. Steel and aluminum could stack on each other. Duties not listed (for example China 301, China fentanyl, reciprocal) remained cumulative [S29].
- Since Feb 24, 2026 the IEEPA tiers no longer exist. Current 232-vs-232 rules come from the individual proclamations (section 3.3).

## 6. Decision procedure (implementable)

Inputs:
- `hts10`: 10-digit HTSUS of the good.
- `origin`: CN, HK, VN, MX, or other ISO code.
- `usmca`: true if the good qualifies for and is entered under USMCA duty-free treatment (Mexico or Canada only).
- `entry_date`: date of entry for consumption (assume Oct 31, 2026 if not given).
- `value`: customs value (USD).
- `col1`: column 1 general ad valorem rate (as a fraction).
- Optional: `metal_weight_share`, `us_content_value`, `is_patented_pharma`, `is_generic`, `chip_meets_232_thresholds`.

Output: list of Chapter 99 lines with rate and duty base, plus total additional duty.

Rules, in order:

R1. Date gates.
- R1.1 If `entry_date` is on or after Feb 24, 2026, apply no IEEPA duty (fentanyl, reciprocal, transshipment, Brazil, India). [S1] [S6]
- R1.2 If `entry_date` is on or after Jul 24, 2026, apply no Section 122 duty. If between Feb 24, 2026 and Jul 23, 2026, see section 4.5. [S6] [S10]
- R1.3 If `entry_date` is before Feb 24, 2026, use the historical rules in section 4 (out of scope for the default path).

R2. De minimis. Ignore shipment value. No de minimis exemption applies to any origin. [S20] [S21]

R3. Base duty. Compute column 1 duty = `col1 x value`. If `usmca` is true and the good is USMCA-originating, column 1 is the special rate (usually free).

R4. Determine the single Section 232 family that applies, using CBP HTS lists, in this order. Stop at the first match.
- R4.1 Semiconductors: if `hts10` is in 8471.50, 8471.80 or 8473.30 and `chip_meets_232_thresholds`, apply 9903.79.01 at 25% of full value. Otherwise 9903.79.02 to .09 at 0% (still counts as a 232 good for R6 only if filed under 9903.79.01; treat .02 to .09 as not 232 for R6, see U6). [S19]
- R4.2 Autos / auto parts (9903.94): 25% of full value. Mexico with `usmca`: vehicles pay 25% on non-U.S. content only; parts pay 0% (9903.94.06). [S14] [S48]
- R4.3 MHDV, MHDV parts, buses (9903.74): trucks and parts 25%, buses 10%. Mexico with `usmca`: trucks 25% on non-U.S. content. [S18]
- R4.4 Pharmaceuticals (9903.04.60 to .70): patented 100% inclusive of column 1 (duty = 100% minus column 1); generics 0%. Use the lowest applicable pharma rate. [S35]
- R4.5 Drones (Proclamation 11055): 100% or 25% by annex. [S36]
- R4.6 Wood (9903.76): lumber 10%; upholstered wooden furniture 25%; cabinets and vanities 25% (30% and 50% from Jan 1, 2027). [S16] [S17]
- R4.7 Metals (9903.82): if `hts10` is in Annex I-A, 50% of full value; Annex I-B, 25%; apply the special headings (U.S. metal 10%, partner top-ups, UK). If the article is outside ch. 72, 73, 74, 76 and metal is under 15% of weight, rate 0% (9903.82.03). If no metal, 0% (9903.82.01). Only one metal rate. Mexico with `usmca`: still applies, except Annex I-C goods use 9903.82.20 / .21 (25% on non-U.S. content and on U.S. content above 40%, minimum total 15%). [S12] [S13] [S45]
- R4.8 If both R4.6 and R4.7 match the same `hts10`, apply both and flag the result as uncertain (U7).

R5. Section 301 China lists (origin CN only; not HK).
- R5.1 Find the list heading for `hts10`: 9903.88.01 / .02 / .03 / .04 at 25%, or 9903.88.15 at 7.5%.
- R5.2 If `hts10` is covered by a four-year review heading 9903.91.01, .02, .03, .05, .06, .07 or .08 that is in effect on `entry_date`, use that rate INSTEAD of the R5.1 rate (U4).
- R5.3 If the product matches an active exclusion (9903.88.69 or .70) and `entry_date` is on or before Nov 9, 2026, the R5.1 list rate is 0%. The exclusion does not cancel a 9903.91 rate unless the exclusion text says so (not verified). [S15]
- R5.4 Add the result. It stacks with R4, R6 and R7. Base is full customs value.

R6. Section 301 Forced Labor.
- R6.1 If any R4 family other than a 0% "not covered" heading applied (that is, the good is subject to a 232 action listed in note 52(f)), apply 0% (9903.05.90). [S10]
- R6.2 If origin is MX and `usmca` is true, apply 0% (9903.05.94). [S10]
- R6.3 If `hts10` is in Annex II Part A, or the country-specific Annex II part, or is a pharmaceutical (note 52(e)), civil aircraft, donation or informational material, apply 0%. [S10]
- R6.4 Otherwise: CN 12.5% (9903.05.31); HK 12.5% (9903.05.43); VN 12.5% (9903.05.84); MX 10% (9903.05.55). Other origins per the CSMS table: flat 10% or 12.5%, or top-up = max(0, target minus `col1`) for EU, Taiwan (10%) and Japan, Korea, Switzerland (12.5%); 0% for countries not among the 60. [S10] [S11a]
- R6.5 Base is full customs value. It stacks with R5 and R7.

R7. AD/CVD. Add any antidumping or countervailing cash deposit for the order covering the product, producer and country. Always additive. [S10] [S48]

R8. Total additional duty = sum of R4 + R5 + R6 + R7 amounts. Total duty = R3 + R8. Fees (MPF 0.3464%, HMF 0.125% for ocean) are separate and not Chapter 99 duties [S22].

R9. Report lines in CBP order: Chapter 98, Chapter 99 additional duties, trade remedies (301, then 232), then the Chapter 1 to 97 line carrying the value. [S10]

### Quick reference by origin (non-232 consumer good, no AD/CVD, Oct 31, 2026)

| Origin | Additional duty |
|---|---|
| China | 301 list rate (25% or 7.5%, or the 9903.91 rate) + 12.5% forced labor |
| Hong Kong | 12.5% forced labor (China 301 lists do not apply to HK origin; not separately verified) |
| Vietnam | 12.5% forced labor |
| Mexico, USMCA-qualifying | 0% |
| Mexico, not USMCA | 10% forced labor |
| Other | Per forced labor country table |

### Worked examples (illustrative)

1. China, plastic toy on List 4A (not on List 1 to 3), not 232, value $100, column 1 0%: 7.5% (9903.88.15) + 12.5% (9903.05.31) = 20%, $20.00.
2. China, stainless steel sink 7324.10.00.10, Annex I-B 25% (secondary example [S39]): column 1 3.4% + List 3 25% + 232 metals 25% + forced labor 0% (232 good) = 53.4%.
3. Vietnam, upholstered wooden chair 9401.61: 232 wood 25% (9903.76.02) + forced labor 0% (232 good) = 25% additional until Dec 31, 2026; 30% from Jan 1, 2027.
4. Vietnam, cotton T-shirt: 12.5% forced labor (9903.05.84), no 232, plus column 1.
5. Mexico, USMCA-qualifying household appliance not in metals annexes: 0% additional.
6. Mexico, non-USMCA same appliance: 10% forced labor (9903.05.55). If it is a metals Annex I-B derivative instead: 25% metals, forced labor 0%.
7. China, upholstered wooden sofa on List 3: 25% List 3 + 25% wood 232 = 50% additional; forced labor 0%.

## 7. Uncertainties and contested points

U1. Section 301 Forced Labor legal challenge. Twenty-five states challenge the action; oral argument was reported for Sept 30, 2026. No ruling found. If struck, R6 drops out. [S15] [S38]

U2. China exclusions after Nov 9, 2026. The 178 exclusions (9903.88.69 / .70) expire at 11:59 p.m. EDT Nov 9, 2026. Trackers report a US China agreement on Sept 27 to 28, 2026 to extend the "Busan" arrangement to Jan 10, 2027, possibly including the exclusions, but no Federal Register or USTR notice was found as of Oct 26, 2026. USTR did publish (Oct 26, 2026) a proposal to extend the maritime fee suspension to Jan 9, 2027. [S15] [S37] [S38]

U3. Vietnam rate. CSMS 69326983 lists Vietnam at 12.5% (9903.05.84), confirmed by two September 2026 trackers. One tracker (Sept 10, 2026) lists Vietnam and the Philippines at 10% and lists Thailand twice. No modification notice lowering Vietnam was found. Use 12.5%. [S10] [S14] [S22]

U4. China four-year review vs list rate. The USTR wording "increase from 25% to 100%" means the 9903.91 rate replaces the list rate. The governing HTSUS U.S. note 31 text could not be retrieved, and secondary sources do not state it. [S9] [S40]

U5. Forced Labor Annex II product exemptions. The Annex II Part A list (and country Parts B to O) was not retrieved. The estimator needs CBP's "Forced Labor HTS list" attachment to CSMS 69326983. [S10] [S11b]

U6. Which 0% 232 headings count as "subject to 232" for the forced labor exemption. CBP said 9903.82.03 (under 15% metal), .13 and .21 were not eligible for the Section 122 exemption. By analogy they likely do not qualify for 9903.05.90, but no CBP statement for the forced labor action was found. [S13]

U7. Wood 232 vs metals 232, and drones 232 vs others. No primary source found on whether these stack on the same article. The tracker [S14] lists non-stacking only for vehicles and among metals. Section 232 drone 9903 headings not verified. [S14] [S36]

U8. Postal de minimis duty method after Section 122 expired (Jul 24, 2026). EO 14388 tied the postal duty to the Section 122 rate; CBP announced a concurrent postal rulemaking. The method in force on Oct 31, 2026 was not verified. [S20] [S21]

U9. Section 122 refunds. The CIT ruled Section 122 unlawful, but the Federal Circuit stayed the ruling. Refunds exist only if the appeal fails. Not relevant to entries after Jul 24, 2026. [S7] [S54]

U10. Canada 35% IEEPA rate and some historical IEEPA EO numbers (May 12, 2025 China EO, Aug 11, 2025 extension, Nov 4, 2025 fentanyl EO) were not verified from primary text. They matter only for refund calculations. [S26] [S27] [S31]

U11. Section 122 Federal Register page citation for Proclamation 11012 not verified (FR Doc. 2026-03824 only).
