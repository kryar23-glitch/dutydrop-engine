# Tariff changes this week: November 2, 2026

The big date this week is November 9. That night, 178 product exclusions from the China Section 301 tariffs run out unless USTR extends them. If you import any of those products from China, your duty can rise by 7.5% or 25% of value on entries after the deadline.

## 1. China Section 301 exclusions end November 9 unless extended

- What: 164 extended product exclusions (heading 9903.88.69) and 14 solar manufacturing equipment exclusions (9903.88.70).
- When: they cover goods entered through November 9, 2026. USTR extended them to that date in a notice published December 1, 2025.
- What changes: after the deadline, an excluded product pays the Section 301 list duty again, 25% on Lists 1 to 3 and 7.5% on List 4A, on top of its base duty.
- What does not change: an exclusion removes only the Section 301 list duty. The Section 301 country duty on China (12.5%, heading 9903.05.31), Section 232 metals duties and any antidumping or countervailing duties apply either way.
- Status: trade reports describe a US and China arrangement running to January 10, 2027, but as of today we have not found a Federal Register notice extending these exclusions. We will update this page when one is published.

What to do: if you file under 9903.88.69 or 9903.88.70, check your product against the exclusion text now and price entries after November 9 at the full rate until an extension is official.

Example: $1,000 of a List 3 product from China that loses its exclusion goes from $125 to $375 in added duty (25% Section 301 plus 12.5% country duty), before the base rate.

## 2. Already in force, unchanged this week

- Section 301 country duties (U.S. note 52): China 12.5%, Vietnam 12.5%, Mexico 10%, in force since July 24, 2026. USMCA-qualifying goods from Mexico are exempt. Goods covered by a Section 232 action are exempt.
- Section 232 metals: since April 6, 2026 the steel, aluminum and copper duties apply to the full value of the article, at 50%, 25% or a 15% floor depending on the list that names the code. They apply to every country of origin.
- IEEPA tariffs: ended for entries on or after February 24, 2026, after the Supreme Court ruling. Refunds of IEEPA duties already paid run through CBP's CAPE process.
- Section 122 surcharge: the 10% surcharge expired July 24, 2026. Its legality is still before the Federal Circuit, with no general refund process.
